\section{Interoperable authorship provenance (RQ4)}
\label{sec:provenance}

The preceding sections establish that detector output is an unreliable basis for
authorship claims in this domain, and that its reliability varies systematically by
genre, length and language. That conclusion has a design consequence: an authorship
claim is only interpretable if the evidence behind it travels with it. A merged pull
request labelled ``AI-authored'' on the basis of a platform account and one labelled
``AI-authored'' on the basis of a detector score are not the same claim, and current
tooling records them identically --- or not at all.

\subsection{An evidence-typed provenance vocabulary}
\label{sec:provenance:model}

\RESULT{Present the PROV-O \citep{lebo2013provo} extension. Core classes and properties:
the human orchestrator; the agent as a tool--model--version triple, since ``Claude Code''
is not a model and the distinction matters for reproducibility; the activity
(generation, review, merge); the artifact chain from prompt through diff and commit to
pull request; and the attribution evidence type --- self-declared trailer, agent
account, platform metadata, or detector inference carrying a score, a detector version
and the measured error rate for that artifact's stratum.}

\RESULT{Figure: the vocabulary as a diagram, with one worked example annotated end to
end.}

The design commitment is that a detector-derived attribution is representable only
\emph{with} its error characteristics attached. This is what makes the model useful
rather than merely tidy: it renders the difference between declared and inferred
authorship queryable.

\subsection{Demonstration}
\label{sec:provenance:demo}

\RESULT{Populate a knowledge graph over the ground-truth corpus and answer competency
questions in SPARQL. The queries should include at least: all merged pull requests whose
authorship evidence is detector-only and whose stratum-specific false positive rate
exceeds a threshold; the distribution of evidence types per repository; and
contributions where declared and inferred authorship disagree. Report graph size and
query timings.}

\RESULT{Optional, cut first if the schedule slips: map AI-contribution clauses from
CONTRIBUTING.md and AGENTS.md files to ODRL policies, making policy compliance
checkable against the provenance graph.}

\subsection{Positioning}
\label{sec:provenance:positioning}

\RESULT{Contrast with SLSA and in-toto. Supply-chain attestation answers ``what build
process produced this artifact'' with cryptographic guarantees, but has no vocabulary
for authorship of content, and no notion of evidence quality --- an attestation is
either valid or not. Authorship attribution is irreducibly probabilistic, which is
precisely why the evidence type must be first-class.}
