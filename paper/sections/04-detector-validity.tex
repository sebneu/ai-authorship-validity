\section{Detector validity (RQ1 and RQ2)}
\label{sec:validity}

\subsection{Detector harness}
\label{sec:validity:harness}

\RESULT{Describe the unified harness: text in, score out; one adapter per detector;
fixed seeds; pinned model and library versions. State the scoring models used for
Fast-DetectGPT and Binoculars (Llama-3-8B class, run on local H100/H200 hardware) and
note that no commercial detector is included, so the entire evaluation is reproducible
from open weights. Report wall-clock cost per genre as a practical note for
replication.}

The evaluated approaches are: Fast-DetectGPT \citep{bao2024fastdetectgpt}, Binoculars
\citep{hans2024binoculars}, \RESULT{DetectCodeGPT}, the heuristic filters reimplemented
from the prevalence studies in Table~\RESULT{1}, an LLM-as-classifier baseline, and the
supervised in-domain classifier of \citet{ghaleb2026fingerprinting} as a ceiling
reference.

\subsection{RQ1: criterion validity}
\label{sec:validity:rq1}

\RESULT{AUROC per detector, per agent, per genre, with confidence intervals. Precision
and recall at each detector's published operating point --- the operating point matters
more than the AUROC, because the prevalence literature applies these thresholds as
published. Calibration assessed by expected calibration error.}

\RESULT{Figure: AUROC by genre and detector, with commit messages expected at the low
end. If the expected pattern does not appear, report it plainly; a null result here is
still a contribution, since no in-domain validation exists at all.}

\subsection{RQ2: structure of the errors}
\label{sec:validity:rq2}

\subsubsection{The counterfactual false positive test}
\label{sec:validity:counterfactual}

\RESULT{False positive rate on N1 with bootstrap confidence intervals, stratified by
genre, language, text length decile and project scale. Every flagged artifact here is an
error by construction. This is the headline result of the paper and the input to
Section~\ref{sec:correction}.}

\subsubsection{Automation as a confounder}
\label{sec:validity:bots}

\RESULT{False positive rate on N3 reported separately, quantifying how much apparent AI
prevalence is pre-LLM automation. Compare against the N1 rate on human-authored text
from the same period and repositories.}

\subsubsection{Text length}
\label{sec:validity:length}

\RESULT{Detector performance as a function of text length, with the matched and
unmatched comparison reported side by side; their difference estimates the length
confound. Given a median commit message of \AidevCommitMsgMedian{} characters, this is
expected to be the dominant error driver.}

\subsubsection{Linguistic proxies}
\label{sec:validity:language}

\RESULT{Error rates against repository-level linguistic proxies, testing whether the
non-native-writer bias of \citet{liang2023biased} reproduces in this domain. Frame
carefully: the unit of analysis is the repository, not the individual, and no claim is
made about any contributor.}

\subsubsection{Agent generalisation}
\label{sec:validity:agents}

\RESULT{Cross-agent results. Since \AidevTopAgentShare{} of the corpus is one agent,
report explicitly whether detectors tuned on the aggregate transfer to the others, and
what a pooled metric would have concealed.}
