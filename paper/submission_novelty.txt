STATEMENT OF NOVELTY — JOURNAL-FIRST SUBMISSION

This manuscript is a journal-first submission. No part of it has appeared before in any
venue. There is no earlier conference or workshop paper, no extended abstract, no
registered report and no preprint. The corpus, the measurements, the analysis code and
the provenance vocabulary are all reported here for the first time, and the manuscript
is not under consideration elsewhere.

Because the paper is not an extension, everything below is new. What follows describes
the contributions and, where relevant, what separates them from the closest published
work by other authors.

1. AN IN-DOMAIN VALIDATION THAT DID NOT PREVIOUSLY EXIST

A body of 2026 work estimates how much of open-source software is written by AI, and a
further literature builds on those estimates. Every instrument behind them was developed
and benchmarked outside this domain, on news articles, student essays and curated code
benchmarks. None had been validated against ground truth on the artifacts it is applied
to: commit messages, pull request and issue descriptions, review comments and code
diffs. This manuscript reports that validation.

The 2026 Mining Software Repositories Mining Challenge produced 62 accepted papers built
on agent-attribution labels within a year. We checked all 62: not one validates those
labels, and not one evaluates a content-based detector against ground truth. The gap the
paper closes is therefore documented rather than asserted.

2. GROUND TRUTH WITHOUT HUMAN ANNOTATION

The study needs no annotators, which matters because whether a text "looks AI-written"
is precisely what is in dispute. Positives are contributions opened by coding agents
under their own accounts, so authorship is declared by the platform rather than judged.
Negatives are artifacts written in the same repositories before ChatGPT was released, so
their composition follows from chronology. Any detection in that set is a false positive
by construction, and the resulting rate is measured rather than estimated under
assumptions.

The frozen corpus holds 86,484 texts across five artifact types and four sets: agent
contributions, pre-ChatGPT artifacts from the same repositories, human contributions
written during the agent era, and pre-ChatGPT output of dependency bots and release
automation. The three negative sets have complementary weaknesses by design, and the
paper reports what each one can and cannot support. Positives and negatives are matched
on length within artifact type, because length is a confound rather than a signal.

Several construction decisions are contributions in their own right, since they are the
kind of detail that silently invalidates this sort of corpus. Negatives are filtered on
the timestamp of the text rather than of its parent artifact, after we found a 2013
issue carrying an agent-written comment from 2026. Commit messages and diffs are read
from cloned repository history rather than from the platform API, because API responses
return current state and an edited body would contaminate a set whose entire purpose is
being free of model output. Generated and vendored files are excluded from both sides
using shared code, and trailer lines are stripped from both sides, so that normalisation
cannot itself become the signal a detector reads.

3. THE COUNTERFACTUAL FALSE POSITIVE MEASUREMENT

Across all 11 combinations of zero-shot detector and artifact type, discrimination falls
below chance. These instruments rank human text written before ChatGPT existed as more
machine-written than text an agent actually produced. Below chance is a stronger result
than poor performance and, to our knowledge, has not been reported for this domain.

4. EVALUATION AT THE OPERATING POINTS THE LITERATURE PUBLISHES

Detector evaluations normally report what an instrument could do if someone tuned it.
That is not how prevalence studies use them: a published threshold is adopted and
everything above it is counted. We apply the operating points published for the
software-engineering domain, unchanged. At its published threshold, Binoculars marks
98.9 per cent of pre-ChatGPT code diffs as AI-written, 92.7 per cent of issue bodies and
51.4 per cent of commit messages. Every one of those is a false positive. We are not
aware of a comparable measurement in the literature.

5. PRE-LANGUAGE-MODEL AUTOMATION SEPARATED FROM HUMAN TEXT

Repositories have contained machine-written text for a decade. Held at a five per cent
false positive rate on pre-ChatGPT human writing, an instructed language model flags
between 71.3 and 97.5 per cent of pre-ChatGPT bot output, a factor of 16 to 21. Asked
whether a text was written by AI, it largely answers whether the text was written by a
machine of any kind. A prevalence estimate produced that way counts a decade of
dependency-update messages as AI adoption.

6. A SUPERVISED CEILING UNDER THE SAME INPUT CONSTRAINT

A detector can fail because the signal is absent or because the instrument misses it.
We separate the two with a classifier trained in domain on text-derived features only,
cross-validated in folds grouped by repository. It reaches 0.982 on pull request bodies,
so the signal is present and the deployed instruments are missing it, and only 0.602 on
code diffs, so the difficulty of code is partly a property of the artifact. The ceiling
deliberately excludes temporal features, which would separate our classes perfectly for
the wrong reason, and sees no more than the detectors it bounds.

7. A TEMPORAL CONTROL THAT QUALIFIES OUR OWN HEADLINE

Because the negatives are old, part of the below-chance result could be the era rather
than authorship. Scored against human pull requests from the agent era instead,
Fast-DetectGPT recovers from 0.295 to 0.460 and Binoculars reaches chance. We report
this as a limit on our own finding. What survives it is that neither detector rises
above chance once the temporal artifact is removed.

8. CALIBRATION

Only two of the seven instruments return a number that could be read as a probability at
all. The supervised classifier is well calibrated; the instructed language model is not,
outside a single artifact type. A study reading an unbounded perplexity ratio as a
confidence is reading something the instrument never offered.

9. A NEGATIVE RESULT ABOUT CORRECTION, WITH THE ARITHMETIC SHOWN

The paper imports the Rogan-Gladen estimator from epidemiology to correct published
prevalence figures for measured detector error. The outcome is not a set of adjusted
numbers. Of 31 detector and artifact-type combinations, 8 give a correction whose
denominator cannot be distinguished from zero, and in 6 more the reported rate implies a
prevalence above one, meaning no true prevalence between zero and one could have
produced that many flags through that instrument. Most published estimates therefore
need remeasuring rather than adjusting. The manuscript works one case through in full so
that the failure is legible rather than asserted.

10. A PROVENANCE VOCABULARY THAT CARRIES THE ERROR RATE

The measurements produce an error rate per instrument, artifact type and threshold, and
nothing in current practice carries that information alongside an attribution. We
contribute aiprov, a small PROV-O extension in which an authorship claim is a
first-class object with its own evidence, and in which detector evidence records the
score, the threshold, the detector version and weights, and the measured false positive
rate, sensitivity and Youden's J of the case it came from. It is populated over the
corpus and demonstrated with five SPARQL competency questions, including which
attributions rest on detector evidence alone in a case where the instrument is known to
be wrong.

11. TWO METHODOLOGICAL BY-PRODUCTS

Reimplementing DetectCodeGPT surfaced two conflicts between its publication and its
released code, which we document and resolve explicitly; our own first implementation,
written before the citation was pinned, scored a different statistic and would have
supported a milder conclusion. We also reproduce the self-admission keyword pipelines of
two of the audited studies from their described construction, which establishes that
this family of instrument has near-perfect precision and almost no recall.

12. REPLICATION PACKAGE

The corpus specification, the detector harness with pinned model revisions, all analysis
code, the vocabulary, the RDF graph and the queries are archived at
https://doi.org/10.5281/zenodo.21976613 and developed at
https://github.com/sebneu/ai-authorship-validity. Every number in the manuscript is
generated by that code, and the manuscript build fails if a figure in the text does not
come from it.

RELATION TO THE CLOSEST WORK BY OTHER AUTHORS

Khosravani and Mockus (arXiv:2606.24429) audit declaration and metadata channels and
find under-counting. We audit content-based detection and find over-counting, so the two
results bound the problem from opposite sides and share no data or authors. Ghaleb
(MSR 2026, arXiv:2601.17406) trains a classifier to identify which of five agents wrote
an already agent-labelled pull request; that is a different task, with no
agent-versus-human decision and no false positive analysis, and we include a variant of
it as a reference point rather than as a competitor.
